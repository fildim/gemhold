# Gemhold: notes for developers

The rules in detail, how the project is organised, tests, builds and publishing. For players, see the [README](../README.md).

A dark-lord tower defense for Windows, macOS, Linux, Android, iPhone and the web, built with **Godot 4.6**. You guard a cave of gems; heroes walk the roads to steal them. It has music and sound effects (the **Settings** button opens a window with separate volume sliders for effects and music and a screen shake switch), and the first lair teaches you as you play: each tip freezes the battle, darkens the screen and lights up the one thing to tap. The first time you have a skill point to spend, the map does the same for Dark Arts. The first time a mine, well or barracks shows up, or a new kind of hero walks onto the map, the battle stops and a tip lights it up and explains it. **Skip tutorial** ends the first-lair tips, **No more tips** ends the building and hero tips; **Reset progress** brings them all back.

The campaign opens on a level map: fifty lairs in five chapters of ten (the arrows beside the heading switch chapter). Lair 1 ("Mossfold Pass") is the tutorial, ported from the web prototype. From there each lair turns one or two difficulty levers further, and later lairs mix them, so no two play alike (see **Campaign** below). Winning a level Brilliant unlocks its night mission. Progress saves automatically: unlocked levels, best rating per mission and skills. Each level guards 5 gems.

## What's in a battle

- **Towers** need their own ground (moss, frost, ash). **Raised tiles** are single tiles of pale, shimmering stone set higher than the rest: any of the three towers can be built on them, whatever the ground, and they give it extra range (Hut +0.8, Shrine +0.6, Crypt +0.5). Which tower to put up high is your call. The first tower of each type costs 50 gold and each more of that type standing costs 15 more (50, 65, 80…); upgrades cost 75 (level II) and 100 (level III).
- **Three ways to attack**, none stronger overall: the **Warband Hut** fires fast, light arrows (best on crowds, blunted by armour); the **Bone Crypt** fires slow, heavy bolts that slow heroes hard; the **Cinder Shrine** holds a continuous beam on one hero that ignores armour and heats up to 5× damage, but starts cold on every new target (best on knights). `tests/tower_test.gd` compares them. Every level starts with 230 gold.
- **Difficulty curve** (fixed, since 2026-11-22): every lair has a `"curve"` in its file, one hero health factor per wave (1 at wave 1, never falling, `Level.wave_curve`), on top of `hp_mul` and `WAVE_HP_SCALE`. The same wave of a lair is equally hard in every game, whatever the player does. It replaced the old hidden **threat** (dynamic difficulty: hero health rose after easy waves, early kills and unspent gold over 250, never fell), which made scores incomparable and punished good play. The curves are that threat as the test bot met it at each wave (`docs/tuning/2026-11-22-threat-per-wave.jsonl`, averaged over 3 seeds, normalised, never falling), with each lair's `hp_mul` kept as it was, so every wave has the hero health the old game gave on average (a margin-matching retune was tried and undone: see the correction of 2026-11-23 further down). `race_hp` was retuned to the 0.9.6 race margins.
- **Veterancy:** towers rank up from damage dealt (240, 800, 1900), up to 3 ranks (+8% damage each). Ranks are hidden from the player: towers show only their upgrade level (a Roman numeral badge). A tower at rank 3 that is also upgraded to level III unlocks its ability: Volley (Huts fire a second arrow), Dread (Crypt hits can terrify a hero) and Lava (heroes a Shrine kills have a 35% chance to leave a pool of burning ground).
- **Meteor** (50 mana): tap Meteor, then the map; a moment later it lands for 650 damage around that spot, through armour.
- **Level IV and masteries:** a level III tower that has learned its ability can become one of two masteries (150 gold), each first unlocked in Dark Arts (`mastery_<id>`), offered as actions in its tile panel; the choice is final. Huts: **Longhouse** (three arrows at three heroes, the extras at `LONGHOUSE_SHARE`) or **Hunter's Lodge** (slower heavy javelin through armour that marks its hero: +15% damage from everything). Crypts: **Ossuary** (stores up to 4 bolts, looses them at different heroes, unchilled first) or **Mausoleum** (bolts burst over heroes close by). Shrines: **Twin Flame** (two beams, each its own heat) or **Sunlance** (burns every hero on the line behind its target, keeps some heat when it switches). A level IV Hut **plunders**: +1 gold on its first kill as level IV, +2 on the next, up to +5 (`PLUNDER_MAX`). The plain level IV step is small (a few points of damage and +0.1 range, `Defs.TOWERS` fourth entries): the plan's larger level IV stats made every mastery about 30% better everywhere before its own effect, against the balance target of +35-45% in its niche and at most 15% elsewhere. `tests/tower_test.gd -- iv` measures each mastery against a battle-hardened level III on a crowd, one Knight and a mixed group (very tough heroes, damage dealt): roughly +30-55% in their niches, +0-17% outside, A and B of a tower within about 12% on average. Tuning numbers: `LODGE`, `OSSUARY`, `MAUSOLEUM`, `TWIN_DMG`, `TWIN_SAME`, `SUNLANCE` in `defs.gd`.
- **Cutting** costs mana. It clears woods, frees **Gold Mines** (gold over time) and **Mana Wells** (faster mana), and razes **Hero Barracks**, which otherwise send extra heroes while waves are coming. The **Cut woods** button clears a random patch of woods for the same mana, without hunting for one on the map. From lair 4 buildings can take several cuts (`site_cuts` in the level file, or `cuts` on one building, rising to 4 by the end); `cut_cost` in game_sim.gd spreads 1.5x/2x/2.5x of the base mana over 2/3/4 cuts, and each cut on a barracks slows its musters by 30%. The building's tile panel shows the cuts as a bar of blocks with "2 of 3 cuts left" (`"cuts"` in its `cell_info`).
- **Heroes:** Peasant, Footman, Scout, Cleric (heals nearby heroes) and Knight (armoured, carries 2 gems), then the **Cutpurse** (lair 4: fast, fragile, carries 2 gems), the **Shieldbearer** (lair 5: other heroes within 1.2 tiles take 35% less damage) and the **Warden** (lair 5: his lantern clears slow and Dread from heroes around him). Chapter II adds the **Herald** (lair 11: heroes near his banner, himself included, walk 35% faster), the **Berserker** (lair 13: below half health he runs 2.1× as fast) and the **Champion** (lair 15: a 950-health armoured boss, never more than one in a wave, who carries 3 gems; when he falls, three Footmen rise where he lay). These support heroes (Cleric, Shieldbearer, Warden, Herald) do not help boats, siege engines (Longboat, Trebuchet, Iron Belfry: `steady`) or flyers still in the air (`GameSim.aloof`); the Seraph, once down on a road, is helped like anyone. Chapter III adds the **Fenwalker** (water does not slow or soak him), the **Engineer** (rebuilds collapsed bridges after standing at the bank 4 s, lays planks over fords he crosses: no slow or soak there for 40 s), the **Rainbringer** (under her cloud Shrine beams never heat past 35% and Lava goes out), the **Longboat** (river roads only; chill and Dread do not work on it; at its landing its crew, `"4f"` or the wave's `cargo`, steps ashore; sunk on the water, the crew drowns and pays), the **Diver** (hidden from towers and the den in fords, collapsed bridges and high-tide water; meteors and sluices still hit) and the boss **Tidecaller** (a wide rain cloud; every 12 s her surge clears chill and Dread around her and lets heroes wade freely; 3 Fenwalkers and 2 Divers rise where she falls).
- **Water (Chapter III):** fords slow and soak, bridges can be collapsed from their tile panel (a flat 15 mana; heroes swim the gap), the **Tide** rule floods fords on a timer (tide clock in the HUD), and **wave routes** send waves down some roads only (the next wave's road starts glow). Two new buildings: the **Sluice Gate** (yours from the start; 35 mana sends a surge down its waterway: heroes in the water are pushed back 2 tiles and take 120 damage through armour, boats triple; 45 s to refill) and the **Dark Altar** (cut free: towers within 2.2 tiles +10% damage and rank up 20% faster).
- **Siege (Chapter IV):** heroes never destroy towers, but three **tower statuses** stop them for a while (`Tower.stun`, `Tower.rubble`, `Tower.silence`; the tile panel names each and its cause, `GameSim.tower_status`). **Stunned** and **buried** towers don't fire and a beam goes out and cools; **Clear rubble** (15 gold, `RUBBLE_CLEAR`) is the first action in a buried tower's panel. **Silenced**: no ability, no battle-hardened damage bonus, 30% slower fire or heating (`SILENCE_SLOW`). **Sturdy Foundations** (tier II, The Hoard) takes 15% per rank off all three (`STURDY`, `GameSim.sturdy_mul`). Heroes: the **Sapper** (`SAPPER`: runs off the road at the nearest tower within 1.3 tiles that is not on a raised tile and that no other Sapper is after, lights a 1.2 s fuse and stuns it for 7 s, dying in the blast with no bounty; slain while the fuse burns, the keg deals 150 to heroes within 1 tile), the **Pavior** (the road behind him is paved, +25% speed, `GameSim.paved` stretches that fade `PAVE_FADE` 30 s after he falls, leaves or turns back), the **Inquisitor** (censer smoke 1.8 tiles wide silences every tower inside it; the den is never silenced), the **Trebuchet** (`TREBUCHET`: rolls until a tower is within 3.6 tiles or 55% of its road is behind it, sets up for 1.5 s, then every 5 s throws a stone that buries the tower with the most gold in it under Rubble for 9 s; never takes gems; once the last wave is out and only engines are left it rolls away, which does not count as escaping, so the win check never hangs on it) and the boss **Iron Belfry** (`BELFRY`: heroes within 1.5 tiles behind it on its road are `covered`, skipped by towers and the den like hidden Divers, though area damage still lands; a Sapper jumps out every 12 s; at the den it unloads 6 Footmen and rolls away; broken open earlier it spills 4 Footmen, 2 Knights and an Inquisitor). Trebuchets and the Belfry are `steady` (no chill or Dread). Buildings: the **Signal Fire** (`beacon`, a hero building: while it stands, waves come 4 s sooner and barracks muster 25% faster; raze it for 30 gold) and the **Siege Workshop** (`workshop`, a barracks that musters Trebuchets; `Defs.HERO_SITES` are razed for a bounty, `Defs.MUSTER_SITES` send troops). The **Dusk** rule (`{"kind": "dusk", "wave": 9}`) turns a day mission to night as that wave starts (`GameSim.dark`, `dusk_t`; the darkness fades in over 3 s); the lair's night mission is dark from the start.
- **The Radiant Host (Chapter V):** **flyers** (`"fly"`: the **Skylancer** and the Seraph) take an air road (`path_info` kind `air`, or one straight line per land road from its start to the lair if the lair has flyers and no air road). Air roads mark no road tiles, so building is allowed under them; `Level.wave_air` deals flyers over the air roads a wave may use. Flyers are drawn `FLY_LIFT` above their shadow, chill holds them at `FLY_CHILL` (half), Lava does not reach them, and gems they drop fall to the nearest land road (`GameSim._nearest_land`). The **Justicar** (`JUSTICAR`): once, below 35% health, a holy shield makes him untouchable (`Enemy.untouch`: `hurt` does nothing) for 2.5 s and heals him to 60%; `fearless` (no Dread). The **Spiriter** (`"chant"`): when he takes a gem he chants 4 s at the cave mouth (`Enemy.chant`) and vanishes with it (lost); Dread breaks the chant and he runs like anyone else. The boss **Seraph** (`SERAPH`): flies; at 66% and 33% health she lands on the nearest land road (`grounded`, she walks from then on), is untouchable 4 s and raises the last 6, then 8, slain heroes at half health (`GameSim.fallen`: no bosses, boats, engines or flyers). The **Chapel** (a hero building) heals heroes within 2 tiles (10 health a second × `hp_mul`) and keeps **hallowed ground** holy: `H` tiles where towers have 80% range and no ability (`Tower.holy`, `GameSim.hallow_on`) until every Chapel in the lair is razed. The **Eclipse** rule darkens the last `dark` seconds of every `period` (`GameSim.eclipse`; `dark_t`/`dark_k` fade the view both ways; `can_dark` decides whether lights are kept). Every lair holds 5 gems (the level format still allows `start.gems` 5 to 7; the owner chose to keep 5 everywhere, 2026-11-15). **Dark Arts tier III** (40 lairs beaten): Legend +5% damage per tower kind (`LEGEND_DMG`), Hoard's Fury +20% den damage (`FURY_DMG`), Dragon's Patience +10 starting mana. Renown (score only) was planned and removed by the owner (2026-11-15).
- **Heavy gems:** a hero walks 10% slower for each gem he carries (`CARRY_SLOW`), never more than half (`CARRY_SLOW_MAX`), flyers too. `GameSim.pace` is the one place a hero's own speed is worked out (base speed, chill, gems); ground, Herald haste and Berserker rage multiply on top. The walk cycle follows it (`Enemy.stride`), so a slowed carrier does not moonwalk.
- **Loose gems:** a slain carrier drops his gems and they float back along the road to the hoard. Any hero on that road (or one joined to it) with room to carry turns toward them, even back the way he came, so kill the carrier where your towers can cover the gems.
- **Targeting:** towers shoot gem carriers first, then the special support heroes (Cleric, Shieldbearer, Warden), then whoever has the least health left.
- **Loot:** a slain hero drops a coin (32%), a mana potion (15%) or nothing, never both. Coins are worth 4 gold plus 1.4× the hero's bounty, growing with the wave number and the square root of the lair's `hp_mul`. Tap loot to pick it up at once; whatever is still lying on the map is gathered automatically when the next wave starts (on its timer or called early). In the last wave drops are taken the moment they fall.
- **The end:** after the last wave, the battle is won the moment its last hero falls or flees (an engine rolling away is not waited for); gems still rolling home count as home.
- **Keyboard shortcuts** (`scripts/core/keys.gd`): pause, speed, next wave, Meteor and cut woods, stored as physical keys in Prefs (`key_<action>`) and changed in the Settings window (click a key, press the new one; a key in use swaps). Not offered on phones. The HUD also shows a boss's name and health over the map, the Eclipse on the tide dial (violet arc) and the Dusk wave beside the wave counter; tapping an Altar, a Chapel or the den previews its reach.
- **The den fights back:** your lair holds charges (1 to start). Each is a bolt it fires automatically at heroes within 2.6 tiles, gem carriers first, ignoring armour. Spent charges refill one every 8 s. The sparks circling above the cave show how many are ready.
- **Ratings and stars:** Survived ★ (you lost gems), Wicked ★★ (none lost, but a hero touched one, even if you got it back) and Brilliant ★★★ (no hero ever touched a gem).
- **Night missions:** winning a level Brilliant (`SaveData.night_unlocked`) unlocks the same level at night, with its own best rating and stars. Opening it on Wicked was tried on 2026-11-15 (the bot wins Brilliant on only 2 of 50 lairs, `tests/brilliant.gd`) and the owner chose to keep Brilliant. Darkness is the challenge, so night heroes have 15% less health (`NIGHT_HP` in defs.gd), or what the lair's `night_hp` sets. You can only build where you can see; the lair, your towers and your mines and wells light the ground around them. A moon on the map marks it: faint while locked, pale when open, gold once won.
- **Dark Arts** (skill tree, on the map): every star is a skill point to spend across the Hut, Crypt and Shrine trees, or on The Hoard: a den charge, +50 starting gold, +15 max mana or +25% meteor damage (Starfall) per point. The screen has one tab per tree (`Defs.SKILL_TREES`; a tree shows once it has skills), each listing its skills in tier sections. A skill may set `"tier"` (default 1); tiers open with the campaign, not with stars: tier II once 20 lairs are beaten (Chapter III reached), tier III once 40 are (Chapter V), and The Land tree once 10 are (Chapter II) (`Defs.TIER_BEATEN`, `Defs.TREE_BEATEN`; lairs beaten = `SaveData.lairs_beaten()`, one less than the lairs open). `SaveData.can_buy` checks the gate; beaten lairs stay beaten, so a refund never shuts a tier. A shut tier shows its header ("Opens in Chapter III") with its skills dimmed. "Omniscient" asks for every rank of every skill (`Defs.skill_points_all()`, 133). Stars come from each mission's best rating, day and night counted separately, so replaying a lair for a better rating earns more points. There is no player XP.

## Campaign

Fifty lairs, in five chapters of ten. Each lair turns one or two levers further than the last: the roads (how many, how long, whether they join), raised tiles, woods crowding the roads, which ground dominates, buildings and how many cuts they take, how many waves and heroes, which hero abilities appear, and hero health (`hp_mul`). Hero health is tuned last, with `tests/difficulty.gd`, so the bot's headroom falls smoothly from about ×3.5 on the tutorial to about ×1.15 on the finale, assuming the player spends about 2.4 stars per lair beaten in Dark Arts (`Bot.points_before`, not capped; `Bot.skills` buys `Bot.SKILL_ORDER` in order, passing over skills whose tier has not opened by that lair). From Chapter III the bot also takes masteries (alternating A and B by tile), opens a Sluice on boats or three heroes in its water, collapses bridges while no Diver is about, and from Chapter IV clears Rubble at once (the first action a buried tower offers) and razes Siege Workshops and Signal Fires, like barracks, before cutting woods (`tests/bot.gd`). It builds closest to the lair first (style `"near"`, the default); `Bot.play(..., style)` also takes `"far"` (earliest along a land road first) and `"mid"` (halfway along one first), which `tests/brilliant.gd` uses. In the dark (night missions, Dusk, Eclipse), when a pass over its spots builds or upgrades nothing while it holds 250 gold or more, it builds one stepping-stone tower on the buildable tile closest to its first unlit free spot, so the light reaches further. Chapter achievements count lairs, not "every lair": "Crusade Crushed" is the first 20, and "Flawless Finale" and "Dawn Never Comes" are earned on the Hoard Throne itself (`Achievements.THRONE`), so they keep their meaning as chapters are added.

## Open and play

1. Install [Godot 4.6](https://godotengine.org/download) (the standard build, not .NET).
2. In the Project Manager, choose **Import**, pick this folder's `project.godot`, then **Import & Edit**.
3. Press **F5** (or the ▶ button) to play on your computer. To start over, use **Reset progress** on the map (tap twice).

**Adding a level:** copy a level file, give it a new `id`, edit it, and add its path to `LEVELS` in `scripts/main.gd`. `LEVELS` is the campaign order; file numbers only say when a map was made, so ids (and players' saved stars) never move when lairs are reordered. The map shows ten lairs a chapter (`PER_PAGE`, `SPOTS` and `CHAPTERS` in `scripts/ui/level_select.gd`); every chapter zigzags left to right, entering from the previous chapter at the left edge and leaving for the next at the right (chapter buttons sit beside the heading). A lair's name gets at most the room halfway to its neighbours on the same row (`Marker.room`): it shrinks a little to stay on one line, and only wraps to two lines (shrinking down to 18) when it must. Optional `intro` story text is kept in the file but not shown. Check the new lair with `tests/difficulty.gd` (below) so it fits the curve.
In the level file, `terrain` uses `m` moss, `f` frost, `a` ash and `L` lair; a capital `M`, `F` or `A` is a raised tile of that ground. Add `"hp_mul": 1.4` to make heroes tougher on a later level. `"night_hp": 0.7` (right after `hp_mul`; over 0.3 and at most 1.0, checked by `level_check`) sets the hero health factor on that lair's night mission instead of `NIGHT_HP` (0.85); `tools/make_lairs.gd` keeps it when it rewrites a file. `"race_hp": 0.6` (right after `hp_mul`, or after `night_hp` if there is one; over 0.2 and at most 1.5) sets the hero health factor in an online race instead of `Defs.RACE["hp"]`; `make_lairs.gd` keeps it too. `"night": true` makes a level night-only (normally night is the unlockable mission). `sites` lists map buildings: `{"kind": "mine"|"well", "cell": [x, y]}` or `{"kind": "camp", "cell": [x, y], "path": 0, "spawn": "2p,1f", "every": 30}` (a `"workshop"` takes the same keys: `Defs.MUSTER_SITES`), `{"kind": "beacon", "cell": [x, y]}` (a Signal Fire), `"sluice"` (with `"path"`) or `"altar"`; any building can add `"cuts": 3` to override the level's `site_cuts`. In `trees`, `T` marks woods. `paths` lists each road's corner tiles; roads start off the map edge and end next to the lair. Waves use hero codes: `p` peasant, `f` footman, `s` scout, `c` cleric, `k` knight, `b` shieldbearer, `t` cutpurse, `w` warden, `h` herald, `r` berserker, `x` champion, then `m` Fenwalker, `e` Engineer, `q` Rainbringer, `l` Longboat, `d` Diver and `T` the Tidecaller, then `a` Sapper, `v` Pavior, `i` Inquisitor, `u` Trebuchet and `I` the Iron Belfry, then `y` Skylancer, `j` Justicar, `n` Spiriter and `S` the Seraph. For example, `"6p,2f"` is 6 peasants and 2 footmen.

**Level format v2** (all keys optional; old files load unchanged, `tests/fixtures/level_v2.json` uses every one):

- **Terrain letters** `w` (deep water) and `o` (shallows) need no legend entry. Nothing is built on water and woods cannot stand there.
- **Water beside roads** (owner's rule, 2026-11-16, checked by `level_check`): around any tile of a walking road (fords and bridges included; river and air road tiles are exempt), at most 2 of the neighbouring tiles that are not road may be water, shallows and deep counted together, so water never crowds out the building ground beside a road. The two allowed tiles are best spent on the two sides of a ford or bridge, so the water visibly carries on across the road. Exceptions the owner decides on are listed in `WATER_BESIDE_EXCEPTIONS` (Twin Bridges and The Flooded Abbey: walking bridges over a river two tiles wide). In `tools/lair_specs.gd`, `"dry"` lays ground over water after the woods are scattered, so the woods elsewhere do not move.
- **Marks** in `trees`: `T` woods, `H` hallowed ground (`Level.hallowed`).
- **`path_info`**: parallel to `paths`, each `{"kind": "land" | "river" | "air"}` (missing = land). Heroes are dealt only over land roads (`Level.land_roads`). River roads must enter the map on water and reach a landing (`Level.landing`). If a wave has flyers (`"fly"` in `Defs.ENEMIES`) and the level has no air road, one straight air road is added per land road, from its start to the lair centre.
- **Wave objects**: a wave may be `{"heroes": "6p,2f", "roads": [0, 2], "cargo": {"2": "2k,1c"}}`. `roads` limits the wave to those roads; its walkers are dealt round-robin over the land roads among them (`Level.wave_walk`; roads under 7 tiles at half a share, `Level.wave_deal`), and without `roads` over every land road, as before. `cargo` maps a river road to the heroes its boats carry (`Level.wave_cargo`).
- **`rules`**: lair rules such as `[{"kind": "tide", "period": 40, "up": 14}, {"kind": "dusk", "wave": 9}]` (`tide`, `dusk`, `eclipse`), read into `Level.rules`. `dusk` must name a later wave (`level_check`); `eclipse` (`{"kind": "eclipse", "period": 70, "dark": 25}`) darkens the last `dark` seconds of every `period`.
- **`start.gems`** may be 5 to 7.

**Road zones:** at load each road is sampled every 0.1 tile and split into zones (`Level.zones`): a **ford** where it crosses shallows, a **bridge** where it crosses deep water. `GameSim._zone_mul` is the one place hero speed changes with the ground: a ford slows to 70% and soaks; a flooded ford (`GameSim.tide_up`) or a collapsed bridge (`GameSim.collapsed`, by water tile) is swum at 40% with no Herald haste; paving (`GameSim.paved`, stretches per road) speeds up 25% (`Defs.ZONE_SPEED`). **Soaked** lasts while in water and `SOAK_TIME` (4 s) after: Crypt chill lasts 1.5× longer and a Shrine heats at 0.6× speed. Lava never forms on water. In races, heroes marked `no_send` (the Champion) are not in the Send shop, and `send_as` names what their kills send instead.

## Leaderboard and accounts

Firebase project `gemhold`: **Authentication** (Email/Password) for accounts and **Firestore** for the leaderboard. Each signed-in player has one document `players/<uid>` with `name`, `total` (sum of best scores), `stars`, `scores` (best per mission; night keys end in `_night`) and `ach` (achievement ids), plus the campaign when `Online.SYNC_PROGRESS` is on: `unlocked`, `best`, `skills`, `skills_t`, `reset_t` and `stats`. Every upload first pulls the document and merges it (`SaveData.merge_online`): the best of each value, the newest whole Dark Arts build (never mixed, so it always fits the stars), and an Erase progress spreads via `reset_t`. A device's save belongs to the first account that signs in on it (`owner` in `user://account.cfg`, kept after sign-out, cleared by deleting the account); a different account gets a clean slate and the old progress waits in `save_<uid>.json` until its owner returns (`SaveData.switch_owner`). `SYNC_PROGRESS` needs the rules deployed; clients from before it remove the campaign fields when they upload, until a newer client writes them again. Anyone can read it; only its owner can write it (`firestore.rules`). Change the rules, then deploy them with `firebase deploy --only firestore:rules`. The web API key in `online.gd` is public by design. Scores are reported by the game, so a player who edits their save could fake one: fine for friends; delete a bad document in the Firebase console.

**Online races** (Hall of Fame → Race): the host picks one of the lairs they have cleared by day (`SaveData.stars` > 0, within the edition), or **Random lair** (one of those, by day), or a night mission they have opened; the guest needs no progress, only the code. A host opens `races/<code>` (5 letters from `Online.RACE_LETTERS`) with `host`, `host_name`, `level` (the lair id tagged with the rules version, `"<id>@2"`: see below), `state: "open"`, `created` and its progress `h`; a guest joins once, writing `guest`, `guest_name`, `g` and `state: "on"`; after that each racer may write only their own progress map (`{wave, gems, kills, score, done, won, time, out}`, `Online.race_progress`). Both clients poll every 2–2.5 s (main.gd `_race_tick`, hall.gd while hosting). Races run with the lair's standard Dark Arts for both racers (`Loadout.for_lair`, the set the lair's hero health is tuned for; without skills nobody could win lair 11 or later) and no tutorial, by day or (`night: true`) at night, and end for both the moment one racer is done: the other is stopped (`GameSim.race_stop`, `stopped: true` in their progress). Each result is counted once in `stats` (`race_wins`, `race_losses`, `race_draws`; leaving early is a loss) and uploaded as `rw`/`rl` on `players/<uid>` for the "Races: most wins" board; on sign-in the larger of local and online counts is kept. `time` is wall-clock seconds since the race began, frozen at the finish (pausing is replaced by Send, so the clock is fair). `out` lists the hero codes sent so far: one per `Defs.RACE_KILL_SEND` kills (like the hero just slain; a Champion sends a Knight) plus any bought with Send (`RACE_SEND_COST` × bounty); the rival spawns the new ones at a road start (`GameSim.race_receive`). `Online.race_compare` decides the winner: survivor > stopped > fallen; survivors by race points (score + `RACE_TIME_BONUS` per second finished before the rival), then gems, then kills; fallen by waves, then kills. Finished races are left in Firestore (a TTL policy on `created` could clear them).

**Race clock and race health.** `GameSim.set_race()` (main.gd calls it as a race starts) turns on `race_on` and the race clock, `Defs.RACE`: the first wave after `first` (10 s), then one every `gap` (12 s) instead of the lair's `wave_gap`; a lit Signal Fire still takes its 4 s off, never below `gap_min` (6 s) (`GameSim.wave_gap`). Hero health is multiplied by the lair's race factor, `Level.race_hp` (`"race_hp"` in the file, else `Defs.RACE["hp"]`). Nothing else about the rules changes. A race takes about 3 to 6 minutes at 1x. The factor is tuned with `tests/race_test.gd`: the bot plays each lair as a race (`Bot.play(..., race = true)`: it calls `set_race()` and stands in for an equal rival, every hero its kills send coming straight back onto its own roads; it never buys heroes) and measures the margin like `difficulty.gd` (bisecting a factor on `race_hp`, seeds 0, 101, 202). Target margin ×1.5, so races are clearly winnable with the rival's extra heroes on top: a lair needs `margin at 1.0 / 1.5`; the default is the median of that over the 50 lairs, and a lair carries its own `race_hp` only when it needs more than 15% different. Tuned on 2026-11-15: at factor 1.0 the median need was 0.53, so the default is **0.55**; 25 lairs carry their own (campaign number: race_hp): 1: 0.9, 3: 0.65, 4: 0.75, 7: 0.7, 8: 0.45, 11: 0.45, 14: 0.4, 17: 0.45, 18: 0.45, 19: 0.4, 20: 0.35, 22: 0.45, 24: 0.5, 26: 0.65, 27: 0.7, 29: 0.45, 30: 0.65, 32: 0.45, 33: 0.4, 36: 0.75, 39: 0.7, 44: 0.45, 45: 0.65, 49: 0.65, 50: 0.35 (24 and 33 after one correction round: one seed was under ×1.0). **Versions.** Races before version 2 had other rules, so a race pairs only clients of the same version (`Online.RACE_VERSION`, no rules change needed): a new host stores `level` as `"<id>@2"` (`Online.race_level_field`) and `race_get` splits it into `level` and `version` (`Online.race_level`; untagged = 1). An old client cannot find a tagged lair and already says "That lair is not in this version of the game: update it". `race_join` reads the race first and writes only if `Online.race_refusal` allows it: an untagged race (an old host) gets "Your friend's game is an older version: ask them to update.", a higher version the update message.

**Connection.** Every race request runs in the background (HTTPRequest) and gives up after `Online.RACE_TIMEOUT` (6 s, token refresh included; other calls keep 15 s). A failed or timed-out poll leaves the race running and the next comes 2.5 s later (`_race_busy` is cleared after both requests return, and they always do). After three failed polls in a row (`main.gd` `RACE_TROUBLE`) the player sees "Connection trouble: still trying…" once and "…" after the rival's name on the badge, then "Connected again" once a poll succeeds. A hero bought with Send or sent by the kills rule is reported at once (`_race_t = 0`) rather than at the next tick.

## Full-game codes

Demo builds (exports with the `demo` feature: GitHub, web, Steam demo) stop after `Edition.DEMO_LAIRS` lairs. A full-game code, typed under **Info** on the map, unlocks the rest for good on that device (`Prefs` key `full`). The game holds only SHA-256 hashes of the codes (`scripts/data/unlock_codes.gd`); the codes themselves are kept outside this public repository. Make more with `godot --headless --path . -s res://tools/make_codes.gd -- 50 "<private codes file>"`, which appends the codes to that file and their hashes to `unlock_codes.gd`; they work from the next published version. A code is not tied to a person, so a shared one keeps working until its hash is removed. When the player is signed in, the unlock is also kept on their account (`full: true` on `players/<uid>`) and unlocks every device they sign in on.

## Graphics

Everything is drawn in code; there are no sprite files. `view/art.gd` (`Art`) holds the figures (towers, heroes, map buildings) and draws them on any canvas, so the board and the icons always show the same picture.

- **Motion (G1):** the rules stay free of drawing, but carry a few view-only fields and one signal: `Enemy.hit` and `Enemy.knock` (hit flash and a small knock-back from Hut and Crypt hits), `Tower.born` (towers squash and settle after being built or upgraded) and `GameSim.burst(kind, pos, code)`, which `view/bursts.gd` turns into particles: dust and scraps where a hero falls, dust when a tower goes up, sparks for loot picked up, debris from a meteor. Emitters are pooled and capped (`MAX_EMITTERS` × `MAX_AMOUNT`). Meteors also leave a `shock` ring and a `scorch` decal (`Fx` kinds) and shake the screen for 0.35 s (`main.gd`, Prefs `shake`, the switch in Settings). Shots leave trails, dropped coins pop up, and floating text pops in and eases out.
- **Icons and frames (G4):** `ui/game_icon.gd` (`GameIcon.make(key, px, lit)`) draws a round framed icon for `hero:<code>`, `tower:<type>`, `site:<kind>`, `skill:<id>`, `spell:meteor`, `ach:<id>` (achievements map to a picture by id in `ACH` and `ACH_PREFIX`) or a plain glyph name. They show on Hall of Fame achievement cards, Dark Arts skill buttons, Guide topics (`Guide.ICONS`) and the Meteor button. `ui/frames.gd` (`Frames.carved`) draws the carved nine-patch the theme uses for buttons and panels. The campaign map is an old map on dark parchment, with ink sketches for each chapter (`level_select.gd`, `_draw_parchment`).
- **Ground and water (G2):** `ground.gdshader` takes both masks as uniforms (`terrain_mask`, `water_mask`: r deep, g shallows) and a `biome` per chapter (0 lowland, 1 the crusade's warmer autumn, 2 the dark, wet marsh; `main.gd` sets `ground.biome`). Water is drawn into the baked ground (deep water dark teal; shallows a lighter, clear blue-green with sand showing through, so they never read as buildable ground); `water_fx.gdshader` adds moving light on one quad over the map (no redraw). Roads over water: plank bridges with rails (broken stumps once collapsed), stepping stones on fords, no dirt track on rivers before the landing. Reeds grow along the shore.
- **Sprites, light and status looks (G3):** `view/hero_sprites.gd` (`HeroSprites`) bakes every hero's walk cycle (8 frames, 1.5× board size, facing right) into one atlas at start-up by drawing `Art.hero` into a `SubViewport`; the board then draws each hero as one picture (mirrored when walking left) on a child layer that sits behind the board's own drawing (`show_behind_parent`) and blends `PREMULT_ALPHA`, because a SubViewport's transparent output is premultiplied. Health bars, chill, Dread, carried gems and the like still go on top. The hit flash draws the same picture again with an over-bright modulate (clamped to white); heroes in the Belfry's shadow are tinted dark. Trebuchet and Belfry are not baked (their looks follow what they are doing). Headless runs never bake, and until the atlas is ready the board draws heroes shape by shape as before. Heroes are drawn 20% larger than their body radius (`Board.HERO_SCALE`). **Night** is a soft light map instead of the old one-pixel-per-tile fog: `GameSim.lights` (den, towers, freed buildings) each clear a soft-edged pool at 4 px a tile, with a warm glow, and the darkness fades in over `DUSK_FADE` after Dusk. The plan suggested `PointLight2D` and a `CanvasModulate`; the light map was kept because Compatibility caps lights per canvas item (the ground is one item, a lair can have thirty towers) and because it shows the build rule tile for tile. Status looks: Rubble is a heap of stones with dust, Stun is smoke with circling sparks, Silence is pale motes drifting down (and no ability ring); a Sapper's lit fuse sparks, an Inquisitor's censer ring and paved road (cobbles that fade) are drawn on the ground; `Fx` kinds `stone` (a Trebuchet's stone in flight) and `smoke`. Chapter IV's `biome` 3 is churned brown mud. The map's Chapter IV page has palisade, castle and siege-engine sketches. Chapter V: flyers have feathered wings (`Art._wings`) and fly above their shadow, flight lines are faint dashed lines that drift toward the den (`Board._draw_sky`, and `ground.gd` draws no dirt track for air roads), hallowed tiles glow pale gold while a Chapel stands, untouchable heroes shine inside a gold ring, a chanting Spiriter has a blue ring that closes in, `biome` 4 is pale sunlit gold, and the map page has chapel spires, suns and wings.
- **Speed (G5):** in the Compatibility renderer every polygon, polyline and antialiased arc is its own draw call, and only textured rectangles from the same texture batch. So nearly everything in a battle is drawn from pictures baked once:
  - `view/ground.gd` paints roads, woods, grass, reeds, the lair and the vignette into a SubViewport (`_over_vp`) at the bake scale and draws that one picture over the shader ground and its ripples; it is painted again only on `ground_changed` (woods cut, bridge collapsed) or a new scale. This alone was about 3,300 draw calls and 15 ms a frame.
  - `view/sprite_cache.gd` (`SpriteCache`) bakes any picture by key: `want(key, box, painter)` queues it, the next batch paints all queued pictures side by side into one SubViewport (UPDATE_ONCE), a second pass (`unpremult.gdshader`, blending off) turns the premultiplied output back into straight alpha and fills clear edge pixels with their neighbour's colour, then it is read back once, mipmapped and kept as one atlas page; `find(key)` gives `[texture, source, box]` and `put()` draws it. Pages are 4096 px wide at Detailed on desktop, 2048 otherwise. Until a picture is baked the caller draws it shape by shape, so nothing is missing for a frame, and headless runs (which bake nothing) always draw that way. `clear()` drops everything (the map is shown, another lair); `renew()` (a new quality or window scale in the same battle) keeps the old pictures on screen until the new ones are in.
  - `view/board.gd` bakes, while the start card is up (`_prebake`): every level and mastery of each tower the lair's ground allows (platform, building, mastery and the frames of what moves: the Hut's banner 8 frames, the level III Crypt's crystal 8, the Shrine's flame 6, firing pictures for each; the Shrine's runes pulse live over it; an Ossuary has one picture per stored bolt count), the level badges, every building of the lair owned and not (with its own loop), siege engines (wheels, arm, bell), gems, coins, potions, shots, den orbs (8 crackles), the "!!" of Dread, gem counts, cobbles, and the shapes everything else is made of: a glow, a disc (circles and ellipses), a white square (rectangles, bars, bands), 14 rings by width, ellipse outlines by size. Drawing then is one rectangle per thing, nearly all from one page, so they batch. Board children, in drawing order: `_fl` (what lies on the ground: hoard, scorch, lava, paving, the tide's flood and planks, raised-tile shimmer, road starts, flight lines, hallowed tiles), `_marks` (heroes' shadows and ground rings), `_layer` (HeroSprites), then the board itself (buildings, towers, hero overlays and health bars, shots, beams, fog, loot, effects) and the bursts. Live shapes are left only where rare or always changing: progress arcs, the selection, fog, floating text, a few short effects.
  - The tide's flood is one soft mask per lair (`Board._build_flood`, 8 px a tile, edges wandering over about a third of a tile and running out into the water beside a ford), drawn in the deep water's colour at the tide's height.
  - Smaller things: heroes and towers are sorted once a frame (towers only when they change); the night fog is built as raw bytes; the HUD sets theme colours, tooltips and the boss bar only when they change; the water ripple shader skips dry land.
- **Quality (`core/quality.gd`, Settings > Graphics, Prefs `graphics`):** Auto (default) is Detailed on desktop and Light on phones and phone browsers, and on any platform steps down to Light once for the session when a battle averages over 22 ms a frame for 5 s (a short message says so). Detailed bakes at the screen's own scale (window stretch times the map's fit, 1.0x to 2.5x); Light at the screen's scale capped at 1.5x, with half the animation frames, half the burst particles (and half the emitters), no ripple shader on water, no twinkling glints on raised tiles (their glow stays) and one flood ripple per ford tile instead of two. Nothing that tells the player something is left out. Settings also has **Show frame rate** (Prefs `show_fps`): a small fps number in the map's corner during battles, for checking a phone. Project vsync is unchanged.
- **Measuring (`tests/perf.gd`, see Tests):** a heavy, deterministic battle in the real scene: 28 towers of every kind, level and mastery nearest the roads, half the buildings taken, 60 heroes kept on the map, vsync off. Scenarios `day` (Frostfang Pass), `tide` (Tidewrack Flats at high tide), `siege` (The Iron Belfry), `night` (Eclipse, in the dark), `race` (The Iron Belfry as a race, no network), `fen` (Blackwater Fen, wide shallows). `off=` switches kinds of drawing off to see what each costs, `shot=` saves one fixed frame (`ford=1` adds planks and heroes on the fords), `switch=` changes quality halfway. On the owner's PC (RTX 3060), 1720x720, 20 s, average / worst 1% / draw calls, before then after (Detailed; Light within 0.3 ms of it): day 33.1 / 43.6 / 4,071 then 4.9 / 8.9 / 65; tide 28.4 / 39.4 / 2,898 then 5.3 / 6.4 / 71; siege 42.8 / 65.2 / 5,176 then 5.6 / 9.1 / 66; night 38.0 / 67.4 / 4,212 then 5.2 / 7.0 / 77; race 49.9 / 64.9 / 5,560 then 6.1 / 9.2 / 85. At 3440x1440, day 34.8 / 125.7 / 4,051 then 5.2 / 8.2 / 65. The atlas is one page of about 3 million pixels at 1720x720 (2 at Light, 11 to 13 at 3440x1440), baked in 15 to 20 ms of main-thread work while the start card is up (70 to 130 ms at 3440x1440); changing Graphics mid-battle costs one frame of about 45 ms while the new pictures are painted.
- **Checking:** frame time was measured windowed at 1720×720 on the PC: a battle averaged 21.4 ms a frame before G1/G4 and 21.7 ms after. Phase 3 (lair 40, 38 heroes and 25 towers, vsync off, 1720×720 on the PC): 30.1 ms a frame with baked heroes, 45.7 ms drawing them shape by shape. Not yet measured on a phone. Check on an Android phone before publishing.

## Ads

Only the free Android build for Google Play shows ads: the **Android Play** preset, the one preset with the custom feature `ads`. Windows, Linux, macOS, web, iPhone, Steam and every demo build have none. AdMob ids and console status are in `Desktop\Gemhold\Play store\ADMOB.txt`.

- **Rules** (`scripts/core/ads.gd`, class `Ads`, the only code that talks to the plugin): one interstitial after every campaign win (`Ads.EVERY` = 1; day and night count, the tutorial, losses and races do not), shown when the player leaves the win card (Next lair, Play again, Map) after the win is saved (`main.gd` `_after_ad`).
